# The world

## The pitch

**Harrowmark** is a cold frontier kingdom at the edge of the known world: pine forests, highland moors, frozen
lakes, a great mountain full of dwarves, and the ruins of an empire nobody talks about. Its people have always
survived hard winters. This winter has not ended in three years.

The old king is dead with no heir, and three powers are tearing the realm apart over his throne. The dead
don't stay buried. The ground shakes at night, a little more every year, and the old fires on the hills keep
going out. Most people blame the war.

## The Harrowfolk week

Seven days: **Kindlemorn, Rimeday, Stonesday, Galesday, Shadesday, Brightsday, Hearthsday.** Kindlemorn is when
the Cradlefires are fed and the week begins; Hearthsday is the day of rest, when families keep the home
hearth. The Hearth Order says the days are named for the old fires. Scholars point out that fire, ice, stone,
wind, shadow and light are six of the Titans' elements, that two are missing, and that nobody remembers why.

## Tone

Somewhere between Skyrim and Dark Souls: a harsh land where people are tough and hope is earned.

1. **Endurance.** Winter, hunger and war are the real enemies. Every victory costs something.
2. **The hearth.** Warmth exists: a tavern, a friend, a fire. That's what's worth protecting, and it's the
   counterweight to the dark.
3. **No clean hands.** Every faction is partly right and partly monstrous. No cartoon villains.
4. **The world doesn't wait.** Things happen whether you act or not. Ignored towns suffer.
5. **A little absurdity.** The world is grim, not humourless. The Rat King is real, and he is ridiculous, and
   he is terrifying.

**Writing style.** Short, concrete sentences. No modern slang. Let characters be blunt. Describe the cold.
Humour comes from people, not from winking at the player.

## The Hearth faith

The Harrowfolk believe the gods live in fire. Every home keeps a hearth burning through the winter, and the
Hearth Order tends the great **Cradlefires**: ancient fire-altars on hilltops across the realm, kept burning
day and night, whose flames by law and custom must never go out. The name is older than the Order: the first
keepers said the fires "rock the cradle", and nobody now remembers what was in it. Letting your fire die is the
worst omen there is. (The campfire you rest at in the game is a small hearth; the Ghost Horror that comes for
those who linger in the dark is folklore made real.)

**The truth the faith has forgotten:** the Cradlefires were never for the gods. Their warmth is what keeps the
Titans asleep. The first keepers knew it. The Order now treats the fires as ritual and the tithes that pay for
them as income, and over the last generation dozens of Cradlefires have gone cold.

The Cradlefires tie the human layer (a corrupt church, a neglected duty) directly to the mythic one (Titans
waking). They replace the Crown of Names from the first proposal.

## The Titans

Not gods in the Greek sense. **Titans are colossal beings of living stone,** older than any people, each fused
with an element. When they walked, they reshaped the land: mountains rose where they slept, valleys where they
dragged their feet. They are not evil. They are weather given a body, and they do not notice small things like
towns.

| Fire | Magma veins in black rock | Olgath | The Emberpeaks |

| Earth | Granite and root, moss on its shoulders | Forgin | Under the Ashwood |

| Chaos | Stone that won't hold one shape | Implex | Titanfell |

**The Ninth.** A secret for NG+ only: Mt. Aegis, the mountain the dwarves live inside, is itself the oldest and
largest Titan, asleep for so long that a civilisation grew inside it. Nobody knows. Not even you on a first
run, although you are closer to it than anyone (see "The answer" in 05_main_story.md).

**What waking does.** A waking Titan pulls warmth out of the land around it. That is the endless winter. The
more of them stir, the colder it gets, the worse the harvests, the more desperate the war.

**How the player meets them: slowly.** On a first run the Titans start as things people mutter about: a tremor,
a cold fire on a hill, a story an old keeper tells. Then lore, then a lead you can Track, then the thing itself.
Nobody explains the Titans to the player up front, and the game never puts one in front of you before the end
of Act I.

## The dragons

In the Age of Stone, dragons were the Titans' rivals and hunters. When the Titans slept, the dragons dwindled
to a few. The **Elder Dragon** remembers the old world and wants it back, with dragons ruling a realm of
sleeping or broken stone. Dragons are rare, ancient and political, not random monsters (the Young Dragons of
the hunts are their degenerate kin).

## Valoria and the Lich

A thousand years ago the empire of **Valoria** in the south tried to *master* a Titan instead of lulling it.
Its last king bound the Titan of Light with the souls of his own people, and for a generation Valoria was the
brightest city in the world. Then the binding failed. The city fell in a night.

The king did not die. He became the **Lich Regent**, still holding his binding together beneath the ruins with
stolen souls. As the Titans stir, his old bindings pull at the dead everywhere: that is why graves won't stay
closed, why Revenants rise, why the Barrowfields are restless. He offers a solution of his own: order through
death (see the Lich ending).

## The Rat King

Under the capital, Brennhold, lies the **Undercity**: centuries of sewers, cellars and smugglers' tunnels.
Everyone in Brennhold has heard of the Rat King, a beggar-king who rules the tunnels, wears a crown of tails
and speaks with a thousand voices. Everyone assumes he's a story the thieves tell to scare children.

He isn't. He's a hive of rats knotted around a throne, old, clever and greedy, and the true master of the
thieves. He buys and sells whatever falls into the tunnels: goods, secrets, bodies, souls. A frozen kingdom
above is very good for business below. (See the Undercity guild and the Rat King ending.)

## History, short

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| The Age of Stone | Titans walk and shape the land. Dragons hunt them. No people yet, or very few. |
| The First Fires | The first keepers learn that warmth soothes stone. They raise the first Cradlefires; the Titans sink into the Long Quiet. |
| ~1000 years ago | Valoria rises in the south, binds the Titan of Light, and falls. The Lich Regent remains. |
| ~400 years ago | Human settlers come north. Dwarves have lived in Mt. Aegis since before memory; elves keep to the Ashwood and the Collegium's tower. King Brenn the First founds Harrowmark and gives the Hearth Order its charter: keep the fires. |
| ~30 years ago | The Order grows rich. Remote Cradlefires start going cold. Nobody important notices. |
| 3 years ago | King Aldric dies with no heir. A Regent rules from Brennhold. The winter that year never ends. |
| 1 year ago | The highland lords refuse the Regent's war tax. A claimant to the throne appears. The realm splits three ways. |
| The game begins | An ordinary night, as far as anyone knows. The ground shakes once, hard, across the whole realm at the same moment: Forgin, under the Ashwood, turning in its sleep. Nobody connects it to anything. Later in the game, scholars start calling it the Waking Night. |

## What makes the player special

Nothing, at first. You start as one more person with a problem (see the origins in 02_races.md), and you walk
into Emberwick like everyone else.

That changes at the end of Act I, when you stand in front of your first Titan. It speaks, and to everyone else
it sounds like a landslide. You hear one word. You can say it back, and the stone listens. Nobody else can say
it; when you try to teach it, they hear grinding rock, or nothing at all.

Every Titan you meet after that gives you another word. The words are a hold over the Titans that nobody else
in the realm has, which is why every faction suddenly wants you in Act II. People start calling you
**Stone-touched**. *Why* you can understand them is never answered on a first run (see 05_main_story.md).
