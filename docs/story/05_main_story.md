# Main story and endings

## Three layers at once

| Layer | The question | Carried by |
|---|---|---|
| **Personal** | Who are you, and why can you understand the Titans when nobody else can? | Your origin, the Titan words, companions |
| **The war** | Who will rule Harrowmark, and what will it cost? | The three political sides, the guilds, the towns |
| **The Titans** | What should be done about beings that could end the world by waking up? | The Cradlefires, the factions' answers, the legendary hunts |

You live mostly in the war early on. The Titans are revealed slowly through lore and hunts. In Act III the two
collide: whoever wins the war decides what happens to the Titans, and you are the one person who can speak to
them.

## The personal mystery

No intro, no prophecy, no "why" up front. You learn it as you go, and on a first run you never fully learn it.

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| Origin and early Act I | Nothing special about you. One tremor in your origin that you'll probably ignore. You're someone trying to get through the winter. |
| Act I climax (the Ashwood) | You meet Forgin, your first Titan. It speaks; everyone else hears a landslide. You hear one word, and when you say it back, the stone listens. |
| Act II | Word gets out. Every faction wants to know why you can do this, and each has a theory (below). Each Titan you meet gives you another word. |
| Act III | The words are your hold over the Titans, and the endings turn on what you do with them. You still don't know why you can hear them. |
| NG+ (the Mountain Wakes) | The answer, at Mt. Aegis. |

**The words.** Each Titan teaches you one word in the stone tongue. They sit in your journal as untranslated
glyphs. Nobody else can pronounce them, learn them or even hear them properly. In play a word is a key (it calms
that Titan's spawn, opens old doors, unlocks choices) and later a rare, costly power in combat. Scholars assume
each word is that Titan's true name. They're wrong, but nobody finds that out on a first run.

**The cost.** Every word you learn makes you a little more like stone. On a first run it reads as a curse.
This is story only and never changes your stats: a stat bonus every word-learner gets is free power, not a
choice, and it would favour builds that are already strong (a mage with stone skin is a mage with free armor).
It shows up in the writing instead, and grows with each word:

- **How you're described.** Grey creeping into your fingertips, then your arms; skin cold to the touch; a voice
  that grinds a little. The character screen's description changes with it.
- **How people react.** Innkeepers flinch when they take your coin; children stare; dogs won't come near you;
  healers notice their spells take longer. Hearth Order zealots get colder; the Grove gets warmer.
- **What it opens.** Lines and choices that only a heavily stone-touched character gets, and epilogue entries
  that depend on how far it went (in the Hearth Rekindled ending, it may be why *you* are the one who stays).

**The theories.** The three explanations from the first draft become things people believe and argue about. None
is confirmed on a first run, and each says more about the faction than about you:

| Who believes it | Their theory |
|---|---|
| The Grove | **Chosen.** The Titans are suffering in a sleep they never wanted, and one reached out to you on purpose. |
| The Collegium and the Dwarven Holds | **Bloodline.** You must descend from the first fire-keepers. The Collegium wants your blood for study; the dwarves check their genealogies. |
| The Hearth Order and the Lich | **Made.** Someone did this to you. The Order calls it a curse to be burned out of you. The Lich says, smiling, that he'd be proud of work like you. |

**The answer (NG+ only).** You're a piece of the Ninth. Mt. Aegis dreams, and it dreamed a small, walking piece of
itself so it could see the world it sleeps under. That piece is you.

- You understand the Titans because their language is yours.
- The words were never their names. They're pieces of *your* name.
- The stone-cost was never a curse. It's you remembering what you are.
- Each new run is the mountain dreaming again, which is why in NG+ you can meet the person you were last time.

This only works if the first run never explains it. Plant clues a replayer will recognise (the tremor in every
origin, the dwarves' oldest songs, the way Aegis's deep halls feel warm to you and to nobody else) and never
confirm them.

## The acts

### Act I: Survive (levels 1–25, about 12–18 hours)

1. **Origin** (race-specific, see 02_races.md), ending with a reason to go to Emberwick.
2. **Emberwick.** Survivors and refugees gather at the crossroads town. Its Cradlefire still burns; the town
   depends on it. You're nobody special yet: you need work, a bed and a way through the winter. The innkeeper
   has odd jobs (see the secret ending).
3. **The realm opens.** The Hearthlands' troubles: wolves, bandits, a frozen harvest, tax collectors. The Iron
   Company, the Hearth Order, the Undercity (through Brennhold's gutters) and the Collegium (through a letter
   or a scholar) each take an interest in you. Guild questlines begin.
4. **The war arrives.** All three political sides court you; none trust you yet.
5. **The cold Cradlefires.** You hear that the realm's fires have been going out, and an old keeper tells you
   what the first keepers believed about them. It sounds like a story.
6. **Act I climax: the Ashwood.** Something huge stirs under the forest and the Grove asks for help. Your first
   legendary hunt, and your first Titan: Forgin speaks, and you understand one word. What you do with it (lull it
   with a relit Cradlefire, kill it, let the Grove wake it gently, or sell it out to the Iron Company) is the
   first big decision, and the realm learns that Titans are real.

### Act II: Allegiance (levels 25–40, about 10–15 hours)

1. **You're wanted.** Word of the Ashwood spreads. Every guild and every side wants you now, and each has a
   theory about what you are.
2. **Guild arcs deepen and collide.** Rival guilds force you to choose (see 03_factions.md).
3. **The Holds.** The Worm is gnawing at Mt. Aegis. The dwarves' questline leads to it as a legendary hunt.
4. **The Undercity.** The Rat King is real. Deal with him, rob him, overthrow him or serve him. This arc opens
   (or closes) the Rat King ending. Late in it, the Iron Company's secret comes out (see 03_factions.md).
5. **The Barrowfields.** The dead won't rest. Following the pull leads toward Valoria and the Lich.
6. **The war comes home.** Emberwick is besieged. Who you've helped decides who comes to its defence.
7. **The truth.** You learn what Valoria did, why the Cradlefires matter, and what each faction really wants
   done with the Titans.
8. **End of Act II: allegiance.** You swear to one side in the war, or refuse all three.

### Act IIIa: The Reckoning (levels 40–55, shared, about 6–10 hours)

1. **Your side's campaign** to end the war, played differently for each side (and grimly for the Unsworn).
2. **More Titans wake:** Ice, Wind and Shadow. The winter deepens; Titan-spawn spread; towns fall.
3. **Valoria opens.** The Warden at its gate, the Lich beneath, the Titan of Light.
4. **Every faction makes its offer:** sleep, accord, binding, killing, profit, a dragon's rule.
5. **The point of no return (around level 55).** One irreversible act sets your ending path (see below).

### Act IIIb: Your ending (levels 55–70, path-specific, about 5–8 hours)

A different final stretch for each path: different allies, enemies, final bosses and closing scenes.

### Endgame (levels 70–80)

Titanfell, the remaining legendary hunts and regional bosses, a world that shows the consequences of your
ending, and setup for NG+.

## Locking

| Tier | When | What locks | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Leanings** | Act I | Nothing structural. Reputations and values shift; small quests open and close. | Yes |
| **Allegiance** | End of Act II | Your side in the war (or Unsworn). Rival guilds were already closed by joining. | Only by betrayal, at a price |
| **Commitment** | End of Act IIIa (about level 55) | Your ending path | No, except the rare deep-requirement turns below |

**Rules.** The point of no return is announced ("there is no going back from this") and confirmed. Locked-out
choices stay visible but greyed with a reason, so players see the depth they missed. Each variant has one
deep-requirement exception that lets a committed player turn at the very end.

## Endings

### The three main endings

| Ending | What happens | How you get there |
|---|---|---|
| **Good: The Hearth Rekindled** | The winter ends. The great Cradlefires burn again and the Titans are sung back to sleep in their own tongue, or (with the Grove) brought to an accord and led away from the realm. Spring comes. But someone must stay awake with them to keep them dreaming: you, or someone who loves you enough to take your place. | Relight the Cradlefires, keep the Titans alive, earn the trust of the Hearth Order or the Grove, learn enough words |
| **Neutral: The Long Winter** | You never solve the Titans. You choose your people instead: one town, one hold, one family survives the winter because of you. The realm endures, smaller and colder. A survivor's ending, and the lone wolf's. | Refuse the factions' solutions at the point of no return, and protect one place above all |
| **Bad: The Breaking** | The Titans wake fully. Mountains walk, rivers move, Harrowmark ends. Whether you meant it (vengeance, power, a belief that the world should change) or failed to stop it decides how the epilogue judges you. | Kill too many Titans (their deaths shatter the land), deliberately wake them, or side with those who would |

### The three variant endings

| Ending | What happens | How you get there |
|---|---|---|
| **The Rat King** | You take the crown of tails. While the kingdom above freezes, you rule the only warm place left: the tunnels. A thousand voices speak with yours. It's horrible. It's hilarious. Business is excellent. | The Undercity questline to the top, a bargain or a duel with the Rat King, and a few choices only a true rat would make |
| **The Lich** | You take the Lich Regent's place, or join him. The Titans are bound again with the souls of the dead, as Valoria did. The realm is safe and silent, and nobody ever rests again. | The Barrowfields and Valoria arcs, a bargain with the Lich; much easier for a Revenant |
| **The Dragon's Pact** | You give the Titans' warmth to the Elder Dragon. Dragons return to rule the sky and the realm beneath them, and you are their voice among mortals. | The dragon-cult and Emberpeaks arcs, sparing the Elder Dragon; much easier for a Dragonkin |

### The secret early ending: A House in Emberwick

The innkeeper at Emberwick (working name: old Brannoc) is just an innkeeper. Talk to him a few times and he
starts giving you odd jobs:

1. **Rats in the cellar.** Clear them out. The biggest one gets away. It was wearing something on its head.
2. **Firewood.** Fetch it from the edge of the Hearthlands before the snow buries the road.
3. **A drunk who won't pay.** Get the money, talk him round, or throw him out.

After the third job he mentions, in passing, that the old cottage at the end of the lane is for sale. 500 gold.
He'd put in a good word.

Nothing marks this as an ending: no warning, no "are you sure", no change in the music. You hand over the gold,
he gives you the key, you walk in, light the fire and sit down. Cut to black.

**THE END.**

One epilogue screen, played straight: you lived a long, quiet life in Emberwick. The winter never ended. You
didn't really notice. Then the credits.

- **The price is the joke.** 500 gold is roughly mid Act I savings (around level 10 today), exactly when you want
  that money for a better sword. Most players walk past it. The ones who buy it get a "what the hell?" moment.
- **It's fair.** The game autosaves before the purchase, so the surprise ends in a reload, not a lost run. The
  ending is recorded in the meta profile, so it counts.
- **It stays open** until Emberwick is besieged in Act II. Buying later still ends the game on the spot, with an
  epilogue line for whatever you left unfinished.
- **Available from the very first run.** Not NG+.

### Special endings (later)

| Ending | How it's found |
|---|---|
| **The Mountain Wakes** | NG+, with every Titan word learned: climb Mt. Aegis and learn what you are (see "The answer" above), and a final ending beyond the others |
| **The Hesitant** | A rare chance in the final scene: hesitate at the climax and the Ghost Horror takes the moment |
| **Fallen** | Every death gets a short epilogue, and that character may return in a later run (NG+ work) |

## How an ending is assembled

So that almost every choice matters without writing dozens of separate finales, an ending is built from three
parts plus epilogue entries:

| Part | Decided by | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Mythic fate** | Your ending path (locked at the point of no return) | Rekindled, Long Winter, Breaking, Rat King, Lich, Dragon's Pact |
| **Realm fate** | Who won the war, and how | The Regency holds, the Free Holds split the realm, Queen Elisande crowned, a Church theocracy, ruin, or you on the throne |
| **Personal fate** | Your values, companions, race and the cost of your gift | Martyr, ruler, exile, legend, monster, forgotten, turned to stone |

Then **epilogue entries** (Fallout-style slides): what became of Emberwick, each guild, the dwarves, the Grove,
your origin's people, the characters you saved or doomed. Each is one short entry with conditions, so they're
cheap to write and make the whole run visible at the end.
