# Races

Harrowmark is a human kingdom. Dwarves and elves are common enough that nobody stares; everyone else is rarer,
and the rarer you are, the more the world reacts to you.

## Who lives where, and how they're treated

| How common | Race | Where they live | How the Harrowfolk see them | Gameplay reactions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Common | **Human** | Everywhere; the Harrowfolk are the realm's people | The default | None; the neutral baseline |
| Common | **Dwarf** | The Holds of Aegis, plus smiths and bankers in every town | Respected, distrusted in business | Better prices with smiths; the Holds open their gates more easily |
| Common | **Elf** | Ashwood enclaves; many in the Collegium | Admired and resented; elves look down on short-lived races | Collegium favour; commoners are cold, some lords are impressed |
| Uncommon | **Halfling** | River villages and caravans | Liked, underestimated | Better haggling and gossip; people talk freely around you |
| Uncommon | **Orc** | Clans beyond the walls; sought as mercenaries | Feared and needed; guards watch them | The Iron Company recruits you fast; some towns bar you after dark |
| Uncommon | **Gnome** | Brennhold workshops | A curiosity | Workshops and the Collegium open up; nobles treat you as a novelty |
| Rare | **Druid** | The Ashwood and the Grove | Pagans to the faithful, healers to villagers | The Grove trusts you; the Hearth Order watches you |
| Rare | **Beastkin** | Northern tribes | Treated as savages, which is unfair | Hard bargains in towns; the north and the Grove welcome you |
| Very rare | **Dragonkin** | Scattered; descended from an old dragon cult | Feared, whispered about | Dragons speak to you differently; some villages won't trade |
| Very rare | **Revenant** | Nowhere. They're the dead who came back | Abominations to the Hearth Order | The Order may attack on sight; the Undercity and the Lich find you useful |

Reactivity is cheap and powerful: a price change, a guard's line, an option only one race gets. A Revenant in a
Hearth Order town and a Human in the same town should feel like two different games.

## Race-specific origins

Every race gets its own opening: about 30–60 minutes with its own place, people and problem. There is no shared
intro and no prophecy. Each origin is a small, personal story that ends with a reason to go to **Emberwick**, a
crossroads market town south of Brennhold where survivors and refugees gather, and where one of the last
Cradlefires still burns. (Emberwick replaces "the camp" as the first hub.)

**One shared moment.** Somewhere in every origin the ground shakes once, hard, at night. Nobody in the origin
makes much of it; the ground shakes in Harrowmark. It's the same tremor in every origin, at the same moment
(Forgin turning in its sleep). A first-time player won't notice. Someone on their third character will.

Characters met in an origin recur later. Your origin also sets your starting reputations and plants hooks that
pay off across the whole game.

| Race | Origin | Inciting incident | What it plants |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Human** | A farmhand in the Hearthlands when the Regent's tax collectors come for the last of the harvest | You defend your family's grain or hand it over; either way your family sends you to Emberwick to earn back what was lost | The war (Regency vs Free Holds) is personal from minute one |
| **Dwarf** | A miner in the deep halls of Aegis | A tunnel collapses during a strange tremor; something huge moves below the mines | The Worm, the Holds' politics, and (much later) the secret of the mountain |
| **Elf** | An apprentice envoy carrying a sealed letter from an Ashwood enclave to the Collegium | Your escort is ambushed; the letter is about the Cradlefires going cold | The Collegium's secrets and elven pride |
| **Orc** | A clan sellsword escorting a merchant caravan across the moors | The merchant tries to cheat your clan; it ends in blood or a bargain, and the caravan limps into Emberwick | The Iron Company, and what outsiders will do for coin |
| **Druid** | A Grove keeper tending a sacred tree that is freezing from the roots up | The tree dies in a single night, and the Grove sends you south to learn why the land is so cold | The Grove, the Earth Titan under the Ashwood, the old ways |
| **Halfling** | A river trader smuggling a crate you were told not to open | You open it | The Undercity, and a first rumour of the Rat King |
| **Gnome** | A workshop apprentice in Brennhold, repairing an old Valorian device for a client who pays too well | The device starts ticking the night the ground shakes, and the client wants it delivered to Emberwick | Valoria, clockwork, the Collegium, the capital |
| **Dragonkin** | Raised in a hidden dragon-cult enclave in the Emberpeaks | The cult's elder hears the Elder Dragon answer for the first time in centuries | The dragons, the Dragon's Pact path, being feared |
| **Beastkin** | A northern tribe hunter tracking a great beast across the ice | The ice splits open under the hunt and the glacier moves | The Frostreach, the Ice Titan, the north's distrust of the south |
| **Revenant** | You wake in a Valorian crypt with no pulse and half a memory | Hearth Order inquisitors are sweeping the crypts with fire | The Lich, the Barrowfields, who you were, and being hunted |

**Phasing.** Ten origins is a lot of writing. Build two or three first (Human, Dwarf and Revenant cover the
widest range), and let the other races use the Human origin with race-specific lines until their own exist.

## Race in the rest of the story

- **Race-only choices** throughout the game: `[Dwarf] "These are Valorian runes. Old ones."`
- **Companions and factions** that refuse or welcome some races (the Hearth Order and Revenants, for example).
- **Race-specific epilogue lines** in every ending.
- **Origin characters returning** in Acts II and III, changed by what you did in the opening.
